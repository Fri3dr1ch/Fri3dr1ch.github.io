Solutions of the KdV equation

Different frequencies travel at different speeds. This dispersion can spread a wave out, while a nonlinear term can steepen it. Their interaction produces oscillatory wave trains and, in special cases, solitary waves that retain their shape.

The Korteweg–de Vries equation (KdV) describes this balance for a real-valued function u(t,x). Choose an initial profile or draw your own, then watch the initial-value problem evolve.

Theorems about the KdV equation

On ℝ and on the circle

Set-up and notation

Let X = ℝ or 𝕋L = ℝ/Lℤ, where L > 0. We study real-valued solutions of ut + uxxx + 3∂x(u²) = 0. On the circle, functions and their derivatives are periodic. On ℝ there is no endpoint boundary condition; the function space specifies behavior at infinity.

Write Hs(X) for the L² Sobolev space, defined by the Fourier weight (1 + |ξ|²)s/2. Here ξ ∈ ℝ on the line and ξ = 2πn/L on the circle. The Schwartz class 𝒮(ℝ) consists of smooth functions whose derivatives decay faster than every power of |x|. The subscript zero in Hs0(𝕋L) means zero spatial mean.

For sufficiently regular solutions, the integral form is

u(t) = S(t)u0 − 3∫0t S(t − r)∂x(u(r)²) dr, S(t) = e−t∂x³.

The zero-mean periodic theory also describes arbitrary mean m: if v solves KdV with mean zero, then u(t,x) = m + v(t,x − 6mt) solves KdV with mean m. Several references use qt = 6qqx − qxxx; their convention becomes ours under q = −u.

Theorem 1. The Airy equation defines a unitary group ℝ and 𝕋L

Dispersion changes Fourier phases while preserving every Sobolev norm.

For every s ∈ ℝ, the operators S(t) form a strongly continuous unitary group on Hs(X). With Fourier convention f̂(ξ) = ∫ e−ixξf(x) dx on ℝ,

(S(t)f)̂(ξ) = eitξ³f̂(ξ), ‖S(t)f‖Hs = ‖f‖Hs,
S(t + r) = S(t)S(r), S(0) = Id.

The function v(t) = S(t)f is the unique distributional solution of vt + vxxx = 0 in C(ℝ;Hs(X)) with initial value f. Its dispersion relation is ω(ξ) = −ξ³, so the group velocity is −3ξ².

Proof. Fourier transformation gives a scalar ODE for each frequency. The multiplier has modulus one; Plancherel and dominated convergence give unitarity and strong continuity. The same calculation uses Fourier series on the circle.

Theorem 2. Linear decay on the line, revivals on the circle Geometry matters

A wave can spread to infinity on ℝ. Periodic linear waves eventually return exactly.

On ℝ. For t > 0, the Airy kernel is

Kt(x) = (3t)−1/3 Ai(x/(3t)1/3), S(t)f = Kt ∗ f.

For f ∈ L¹(ℝ) ∩ L²(ℝ) and t ≠ 0, with an absolute constant C,

‖S(t)f‖L∞(ℝ) ≤ C|t|−1/3 ‖f‖L¹(ℝ).

On 𝕋L. For every f ∈ Hs(𝕋L),

S(TL)f = f, TL = L³/(4π²).

Consequently, there is no corresponding global-in-time L¹ → L∞ estimate with a factor tending to zero on the circle, even after removing the constant mode.

Proof. Rescale the Fourier integral and use boundedness of Ai on ℝ for the line estimate. At TL, every periodic multiplier is exp(2πin³) = 1. A nonzero smooth zero-mean function contradicts a decaying estimate at successive revivals. Airy estimates and smoothing are developed in [1].

Theorem 3. Global well-posedness down to H−1 ℝ and 𝕋L

The nonlinear flow continues for all times, including for initial data much rougher than a drawn curve.

The classical KdV flow extends uniquely to a jointly continuous group

Φ : ℝ × H−1(X) → H−1(X).

For every u0 ∈ H−1(X) and every smooth approximating sequence u0,n → u0 in H−1 (Schwartz on ℝ), its classical solutions satisfy, for every finite T,

un → Φ(·,u0) in C([−T,T];H−1(X)).

The limit is independent of the approximation. This is uniqueness of the continuous extension of the classical flow; it does not assert uniqueness among every conceivable H−1 weak solution. At this regularity the expression u² requires care.

Proof references. Killip–Vişan [2], Theorem 1.1 and the discussion of the solution concept. The periodic result was first proved by Kappeler–Topalov [3]. The simulation’s time limit is unrelated to any breakdown of this flow.

Theorem 4. Unconditional uniqueness in L² ℝ and 𝕋L

At L² regularity, the solution is determined without imposing an auxiliary Fourier restriction norm.

For every real u0 ∈ L²(X), KdV has a global solution u ∈ C(ℝ;L²(X)). If u,v ∈ C([−T,T];L²(X)) are distributional solutions with the same initial data, then

u(t) = v(t) for every t ∈ [−T,T].

Here u² ∈ C([−T,T];L¹(X)), so its spatial derivative is a distribution. “Unconditional” refers to uniqueness in the full stated solution class, with no extra space-time assumption. It is a stronger uniqueness statement than the approximation-based assertion in Theorem 3.

Proof references. On ℝ: Zhou [4]. On the circle: Babin–Ilyin–Titi [5], Theorem 6.4 and the normal-form equations preceding it. Arbitrary periodic mean is handled by the change of variables in the set-up.

Theorem 5. Three conservation laws and Hamiltonian form ℝ and 𝕋L

The dispersive term redistributes a profile without dissipating its conserved quantities.

Let u be a smooth periodic solution, or a solution in C(ℝ;𝒮(ℝ)). Then

M(u) = ∫X u dx, P(u) = ∫X u² dx,
H(u) = ∫X (½ux² − u³) dx

are independent of time, and KdV is the Hamiltonian evolution

ut = ∂x(δH/δu), δH/δu = −uxx − 3u².

Conservation of P also holds for the global L² flow by smooth approximation. The integral M need not exist for general L² data on ℝ, and H is not defined for arbitrary H−1 data.

Proof. Differentiate the integrals, substitute KdV, and integrate by parts. For H, use dH/dt = ∫ (δH/δu)∂x(δH/δu) dx = 0. The displayed diagnostics approximate exactly these three functionals.

Theorem 6. Solitary and cnoidal traveling waves ℝ and 𝕋L

The circle has exact periodic traveling waves of its own.

On ℝ. For c > 0, the profile

Qc(x) = (c/2) sech²(√c x/2)

gives the exact solution u(t,x) = Qc(x − ct − x0). Its height is c/2 and width is proportional to c−1/2.

On the circle. Let 0 < m < 1, κ > 0, and a ∈ ℝ. With Jacobi’s elliptic cosine cn and complete elliptic integral K,

u(t,x) = a + 2κ²m cn²(κ(x − ct − x0);m),
c = 6a + 4κ²(2m − 1), L = 2K(m)/κ,

is an exact L-periodic solution. For fixed κ and a = 0, the limit m ↑ 1 yields the real-line soliton of speed 4κ², while its period tends to infinity. The simulator’s soliton presets on the circle are periodized real-line profiles, rather than exact cnoidal profiles.

Proof. Set u(t,x) = F(x − ct) and integrate once: F″ − cF + 3F² = constant. Substitute the stated formulas, using (cn²)″ = 2(1 − m) + 4(2m − 1)cn² − 6m cn⁴.

Theorem 7. The Lax equation preserves the spectrum ℝ and 𝕋L

A nonlinear PDE becomes an isospectral deformation of a one-dimensional Schrödinger operator.

For a smooth periodic or Schwartz solution, define

Lu = −∂x² − u, Bu = −4∂x³ − 6u∂x − 3ux.

Then KdV is equivalent to

∂tLu = [Bu,Lu], [B,L] = BL − LB.

The associated Schrödinger operators are unitarily conjugate along the flow. On ℝ, the negative eigenvalues stay fixed. For periodic potentials, the Floquet discriminant, hence every spectral band edge and gap, stays fixed. Isospectrality is one part of integrability; Theorems 8 and 10 give the corresponding coordinates for the evolution.

Proof references. Lax [6]. Directly expanding the commutator verifies the equation; the skew-adjoint evolution generated by B gives the conjugacy. The signs above correspond to q = −u.

Theorem 8. Inverse scattering solves the decaying-data problem ℝ

Scattering coordinates evolve explicitly, although u itself does not.

For real u0 ∈ 𝒮(ℝ), the scattering data of −∂x² − u determine the solution uniquely by inverse scattering. With right reflection coefficient R, eigenvalues −κj², and positive norming constants γj,

R(k,t) = e8ik³tR(k,0), κj(t) = κj(0),
γj(t) = e4κj³tγj(0).

Zero reflection coefficient gives pure multi-soliton solutions; nonzero reflection carries radiation.

Proof references. Gardner–Greene–Kruskal–Miura [7]; the scattering convention and time evolution above are recorded in [8], §2, Lemma 2.2.

Theorem 9. Soliton resolution in the right-moving region ℝ · Schwartz data

The persistent right-moving pulses are selected by the discrete spectrum.

Let u0 ∈ 𝒮(ℝ) be real. Write the finitely many negative eigenvalues of −∂x² − u0 as −κj². There are phases xj+ such that, for every ε > 0,

limt→∞ supx≥εt |u(t,x) − ∑j=1N Q4κj²(x − 4κj²t − xj+)| = 0.

Thus each eigenvalue produces a soliton of speed 4κj². Radiation has separate asymptotics in the left-moving and transition regions. This is a statement in a specified spatial region and norm, not global L² convergence to solitons alone.

Proof reference. Grunert–Teschl [8], Theorem 4.4, specialized to Schwartz data and translated by q = −u. Classical KdV soliton asymptotics under these hypotheses are established; the periodic counterpart is Theorem 11.

Theorem 10. Global Birkhoff coordinates 𝕋L

A nonlinear Fourier transform turns periodic KdV into independent rotations with frequencies depending on the conserved actions.

For every s ≥ 0, there is a compatible real-analytic canonical diffeomorphism

Ω : Hs0(𝕋L) → hs+1/2,
hα = {z = (xn,yn)n≥1 : ∑n≥1 n2α(xn² + yn²) < ∞}.

In these coordinates, with an orientation chosen for the angles,

In = ½(xn² + yn²), İn = 0,
zn(t) = eiωn(I)tzn(0), zn = xn + iyn.

For s ≥ 1, the Hamiltonian in these coordinates depends only on I. This gives a precise global meaning of complete integrability on the circle.

Proof references. [9], Theorem 1.1 and the flow in Birkhoff coordinates. The distribution-space extension and interpolation are in [10]. These formulas are stated in the smooth-enough range needed here.

Corollary 11. Almost-periodic dynamics and finite-gap approximation 𝕋L

On the circle, the long-time description is recurrent motion on invariant tori.

For real u0 ∈ Hs0(𝕋L), s ≥ 0, the orbit is relatively compact in Hs, and t ↦ u(t) is Bohr almost periodic: for every ε > 0, there is R > 0 such that every interval of length R contains a time shift τ with

supt∈ℝ ‖u(t + τ) − u(t)‖Hs < ε.

Truncating the Birkhoff coordinates of the initial data gives finite-gap initial profiles converging in Hs. Their solutions have finitely many active angles and are quasi-periodic in time (possibly periodic). This approximation holds on each bounded time interval; it need not be uniform for all time because frequencies change.

Proof from Theorem 10. Fixed actions give uniformly small tails and rotations on compact tori. Approximate the original orbit by finitely many rotations at its own frequencies and apply continuity of Ω−1. This proves almost periodicity. Continuity of the KdV flow gives the stated finite-gap approximation. Positive-time returns preclude a general decay-to-zero statement on the circle.

Open questions about KdV and its relatives

Literature checked 7 November 2026

Complete integrability leaves substantial questions about rough solutions, spectral coordinates, and long-time statistics. Other problems arise when the coefficients or the nonlinearity change. The first six entries concern classical KdV itself; the remaining five concern related equations.

These are conjectures or research problems, not theorems. Each entry specifies its scope and the progress found in the literature check. “No resolution located” records the outcome of that check; recent preprint claims are identified separately. None of the generalized equations below is simulated above.

Classical KdV · circle · conjecture

1. The sharp L⁸ estimate for periodic Airy evolution

Even the linear group has an unresolved arithmetic estimate.

Let 𝕋 = ℝ/ℤ. For every ε > 0, does a constant Cε exist such that, for all integers N ≥ 1 and all complex coefficients an,

‖∑|n|≤N an e2πi(nx+n³t)‖L⁸(𝕋²) ≤ CεNε(∑|n|≤N|an|²)1/2?

Time is rescaled here to one linear revival period. The estimate asks for almost no derivative loss. It concerns arbitrary coefficients, not just an unweighted exponential sum.

Known and remaining. Bourgain proved the analogous L⁶ bound with an ε-loss. Hughes–Wooley established the conjectured scale at L¹⁰. The critical L⁸ estimate remains a stated conjecture in the literature checked, including a June 2026 preprint.

Sources. [R1], Introduction, Conjecture 1.1 and Theorem 1.2; [R2], Introduction, Conjecture 1.2. No L⁸ resolution was located.

Classical KdV · ℝ and circle · open uniqueness problem

2. Intrinsic uniqueness at H⁻¹ regularity

The unique continuous extension of a smooth flow need not settle uniqueness for an intrinsic notion of rough solution.

Are green solutions in C([−T,T];H−1(X)) uniquely determined by their initial value? This solution concept replaces the undefined square of a general H⁻¹ distribution with a resolvent identity.

In the convention q = −u, let gκ be the diagonal Green’s function of (−∂x² + q + κ²)−1. The defining identity, for every sufficiently large κ, is

1/(2gκ(t)) − 1/(2gκ(0)) = ∂x∫0t (q(r) − 2κ²)/gκ(r) dr.

It is meaningful in CtH−2x. The question fixes this solution concept rather than leaving “weak solution” unspecified.

Known and remaining. Uniqueness holds in CtL²x. The H⁻¹ green-solution question is explicitly left open by Killip–Murphy–Vişan and reiterated by Chapouto–Killip–Vişan. The May 2026 Klaus–Koch–Liu revision proves further weak uniqueness results under additional L², smoothing, and tail hypotheses; it does not settle this question.

Sources. [2], Proposition 3.1, for the resolvent identity; [R3], Definition 7.3; [R4], Introduction; [R5], Theorems 1.1, 2.7, and 2.24. No resolution for the stated class was located.

Classical KdV · ℝ · research program

3. Spectral coordinates for general Sobolev data

The circle has a global nonlinear Fourier transform. An equally complete transform on the line is missing outside special decay classes.

Construct an invertible nonlinear spectral transform for general real data in Hs(ℝ), for a fixed s ≥ −1 and without additional spatial decay, that conjugates KdV to explicit spectral evolution:

𝒯(Φtu0) = Rt𝒯(u0).

Here Rt should be a simple evolution in the new coordinates, as in inverse scattering for rapidly decaying data. A complete theory must account for slow decay and exceptional spectral components, including threshold or embedded eigenvalues and singular continuous spectrum where they occur.

Known and remaining. Killip–Vişan’s ICM 2026 account explicitly identifies this missing coordinate system for general Sobolev data. The issue already appears for smooth potentials with only algebraic decay. This is a program for extending integrability theory, not a claim that the Sobolev Cauchy problem is unsolved.

Source. [R6], §3, discussion of inverse scattering and general Hs(ℝ) data.

Classical KdV · ℝ with spatially quasiperiodic data · open existence problem

4. Global existence for large analytic quasiperiodic data

Replacing one spatial period by several incommensurate frequencies changes the existence problem.

Let u0(x) = F(ωx), with F real analytic on a d-dimensional torus, d ≥ 2, and a Diophantine frequency vector:

|n · ω| ≥ γ|n|−τ for every 0 ≠ n ∈ ℤd,

for some γ,τ > 0. Without a smallness assumption on F, does the classical KdV solution exist for all time and remain bounded in x on each bounded time interval?

Known and remaining. Damanik–Goldstein prove local analytic theory for arbitrary size and global theory for sufficiently small analytic coefficients. Spencer’s 2024 lecture explicitly identifies the large-data question as open. No later general resolution was located. This concerns quasiperiodicity in space; periodic solutions that are quasiperiodic in time do not answer it.

Sources. [R7], Abstract and main results; [R8], introductory slides and slide 13.

Classical KdV · ℝ · Conjecture 7.6 of Killip–Murphy–Vişan

5. Does the white-noise KdV flow mix in time?

An invariant distribution need not forget correlations between the initial state and its later evolution.

Let μ be spatial white-noise measure on ℝ and Φt its almost-sure deterministic KdV flow. For real F,G ∈ L²(μ), is

limt→∞ ∫ F(Φtq)G(q) dμ(q) = (∫ F dμ)(∫ G dμ)?

White noise here is a random distribution with covariance 𝔼[⟨q,f⟩⟨q,g⟩] = ∫ fg dx for real test functions. The conjecture asks for mixing under time evolution.

Known and remaining. Existence of the flow and invariance of μ are proved. Temporal mixing is the authors’ explicit conjecture. Mixing under spatial translations, or for a forced and damped KdV equation, is a different assertion. No temporal-mixing resolution for this deterministic whole-line flow was located.

Source. [R3], Introduction and Conjecture 7.6.

Classical KdV · circle · global nondegeneracy problem

6. Can the action-frequency map degenerate at an infinite-gap action?

Global Birkhoff coordinates exist, but the dependence of their frequencies on the actions is not completely understood.

On the unit circle, subtract the linear part of the KdV Hamiltonian in action coordinates:

H*(I) = HKdV(I) − ∑n≥1(2πn)³In.

This renormalized function extends analytically to ℓ²+ = {I ∈ ℓ² : In ≥ 0}. Does every such I admit c(I) > 0 with

−D²H*(I)[J,J] ≥ c(I)‖J‖ℓ²² for all J ∈ ℓ²?

This asks for a coercive Hessian, a precise nondegeneracy property stronger than some uses of the phrase “strict concavity.”

Known and remaining. Kappeler–Molnar prove this on an open dense set containing the origin. A 25 October 2026 preprint reports coercivity at every finite-gap action, meaning only finitely many nonzero actions. That recent claim does not cover arbitrary infinite-gap actions; no full resolution was located.

Sources. [R9], Introduction, Theorem 1.3 and §3.2; [R10], abstract. The latter is recorded as recent preprint progress.

KdV with variable coefficients · ℝ · research problem

7. Does sharp H⁻¹ well-posedness survive localized perturbations?

A small imperfection can remove exact conservation without changing the leading dispersive term.

Consider real smooth coefficients and

ut + uxxx + 6uux = ∂x(a1ux) + a2u² + a3ux + a4u.

Assume |aj| + |∂xaj| ≤ ε/(1+x²) for j = 1,2,3, with small ε, and the analogous bound with an arbitrary fixed constant for a4, uniformly on a time interval. Can one construct a locally well-posed solution map for all real H−1(ℝ) data, as a unique continuous extension of the smooth solution map?

Known and remaining. Ifrim–Laurens (2026) prove H⁻¹ a priori bounds for this localized coefficient class. Such bounds control existing smooth solutions; they do not by themselves provide existence, uniqueness, and continuous dependence at H⁻¹. Upgrading this estimate to the stated solution map is a research target; no general theorem for this class was located.

Source. [R11], Introduction, Theorem 1.1 and the comparison with existing well-posedness results. The sign of a2 is relabelled when changing from q to u.

Changing the power: generalized KdV

The next four entries concern ut + ∂x(uxx + up) = 0 on ℝ, with p = 4 or p = 5. They are non-integrable relatives of the quadratic equation simulated above. Their thresholds and solitary waves use this normalization.

Focusing quintic gKdV · ℝ · scattering conjecture

8. Does every solution below the ground-state mass scatter?

The solitary wave supplies a natural threshold, but the full below-threshold scattering theorem is still missing.

For p = 5, let Q be the positive decaying solution of Q″ + Q⁵ = Q. Does every real u0 ∈ L²(ℝ) satisfying ‖u0‖2 < ‖Q‖2 generate a global solution with some u± ∈ L²(ℝ) such that

limt→±∞ ‖u(t) − S(t)u±‖2 = 0?

This is scattering to the linear Airy group. The strict mass inequality excludes the solitary wave itself.

Known and remaining. This is an explicit conjecture in Killip–Kwon–Shao–Vişan and Dodson–Gavrus. The latter prove a specific instability result near Q, not the full conjecture. Defocusing quintic scattering is solved; it does not settle the focusing equation. No general focusing resolution was located.

Sources. [R12], Introduction, Conjecture 1.4; [R13], Introduction, Conjecture 1.2.

Quartic gKdV · ℝ · general soliton-resolution problem

9. Soliton resolution for general localized initial data

Can a general profile be understood asymptotically without assuming it starts near a solitary wave?

For p = 4 and arbitrary real Schwartz initial data, establish a complete description of u(t) as t → +∞ in terms of separated solitary waves and dispersive radiation. With Q now the positive decaying solution of the quartic profile equation, the solitary waves are

Qc(x) = c1/3Q(√c x), Q″ + Q⁴ = Q, c > 0.

A full theorem must specify the convergence regions and norms and address the possibility of arbitrarily small outgoing solitons. It should not assume in advance that the solution remains close to a soliton train.

Known and remaining. Small-data scattering and perturbative stability near a solitary wave are established, including work of Tao and Koch–Marzuola. These results do not give a resolution theorem for arbitrary localized profiles. No general theorem was located. Classical quadratic KdV with rapidly decaying data is already treated by inverse scattering, as in Theorem 9 above.

Sources and partial results. [R14], Introduction and scattering theorem; [R15], Introduction and stability theorem; [R16], discussion of non-integrable soliton dynamics.

Quartic gKdV · ℝ · collision conjecture

10. Must every two-soliton collision be inelastic?

KdV’s elastic collisions are special. Proving their complete absence for quartic gKdV is still a separate problem.

For p = 4, prove that no solution is a pure two-soliton in both time directions, for every incoming speed ratio 0 < c2/c1 < 1. “Pure” means that, for appropriate speeds and shifts at each end,

‖u(t) − ∑j=12 Qcj±(· − cj±t − xj±)‖H¹ → 0 as t → ±∞.

Thus there would be no nonzero radiation remainder at either end. The question is whether such a collision is impossible.

Known and remaining. Martel–Merle prove inelasticity for very unequal speeds. Later work proves nonexistence of pure two-solitons for the explicit range 3c1/4 < c2 < c1. The all-ratios assertion is stated as a conjecture in their introduction; no complete later resolution was located.

Sources. [R16], main collision theorem; [R17], Introduction, conjecture before §1.2 and the main nonexistence theorem.

Focusing quintic gKdV · ℝ · explicit 2026 open problems

11. Which finite-point blow-up dynamics can occur?

Constructing new concentration rates raises a sharper classification and stability problem.

For p = 5, consider H¹(ℝ) solutions that concentrate at a finite spatial point at time T, with

‖ux(t)‖2 ∼ C(T − t)−ν, C > 0.

Which power exponents ν are possible? In particular, do finite-energy examples exist in (1/3,2/5) or (2/5,3/7]? Do the newly constructed finite-point regimes have any stability? Can non-power-law rates oscillate between two admissible finite-point powers?

Known and remaining. Martel–Pilod’s January 2026 preprint constructs all finite-point exponents 3/7 < ν < 1/2, supplementing the earlier ν = 2/5 example. Its §1.3 explicitly leaves the missing exponents, stability, and more complicated rates open. This statement does not claim that the endpoint ν = 1/2 is settled, or conflate finite-point blow-up with concentration escaping to infinity.

Source. [R18], Theorem 1.1 and §1.3(2)–(4). No subsequent resolution of these listed questions was located.

Questions excluded because they are already settled

What the computation does

Fourier evolution

The solver uses a Fourier pseudospectral discretization and fourth-order exponential time differencing (ETDRK4). The linear dispersive part is integrated through its exact Fourier multiplier; the quadratic term uses the two-thirds dealiasing rule. The default step is Δt = 0.004, halved at high resolution.

Drawing is projected onto the retained Fourier modes. Additional smoothing suppresses unresolved corners. The evolved initial profile is the smoothed curve shown in gray.

Kassam & Trefethen (2005): ETDRK4 ↗

Domains and accuracy

Periodic mode computes on [−40, 40). By default, real-line mode embeds a tapered profile in [−640, 640), with sixteen times as many grid points; only [−40, 40] is shown. Advanced settings offers box lengths 640, 1,280, 2,560, and 5,120 and time limits 20, 60, and 100. Grid spacing and time step are preserved when enlarging the box. This is a periodic-box approximation to a non-periodic problem, without absorbing boundaries.

The simulation pauses if appreciable L² mass reaches the outer buffer. This check cannot rule out all finite-box errors. Compare resolutions and conservation drift when judging a picture. Drift is reported as |I(t) − I(0)| / max(1, |I(0)|); conservation alone does not establish convergence.
