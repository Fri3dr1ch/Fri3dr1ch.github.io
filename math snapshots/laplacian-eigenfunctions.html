Eigenfunctions of the planar Laplacian

For a bounded, connected domain Ω ⊂ ℝ², consider the eigenvalue problem −Δu = λu in Ω. Choose either the Dirichlet condition u = 0 on ∂Ω, or the Robin condition ∂nu + αu = 0, where n is the outward unit normal. The choice α = 0 gives Neumann conditions.

Set-up and notation

A domain is a nonempty, connected, open subset of ℝ². Write A = |Ω| for area, P = |∂Ω| for perimeter, ν for the outward unit normal, and dσ for boundary arclength. Unless a statement explicitly uses weaker hypotheses, the domain is bounded with C∞ boundary. A smooth boundary need not be connected.

The real Sobolev space H¹(Ω) consists of square-integrable functions with square-integrable weak first derivatives; H¹₀(Ω) is the closure of Cc∞(Ω) in H¹(Ω). The trace operator is denoted by γ. Define the forms

qDΩ(u,v)=∫Ω∇u·∇vdxqαΩ(u,v)=∫Ω∇u·∇vdx+α∫∂Ωγuγvdσq_D^\Omega(u,v)=\int_\Omega\nabla u\cdot\nabla v\,dx,\quad q_\alpha^\Omega(u,v)=\int_\Omega\nabla u\cdot\nabla v\,dx+\alpha\int_{\partial\Omega}\gamma u\,\gamma v\,d\sigma.

The operators are the self-adjoint operators associated with these closed, lower-semibounded forms. We write λjD, λjN, and λj(α) = λjRα, always in nondecreasing order and repeated according to multiplicity, starting at j = 1. A superscript B means one fixed choice among these conditions. Inner products and norms without a boundary subscript are in L²(Ω).

Here eigenfunctions are normalized in L². The plot instead rescales each function to max |u| = 1. Polygonal examples have Lipschitz boundaries but fail the smooth-boundary hypothesis at their vertices; the qualifications in Theorem 2 are therefore relevant.

Theorem 1. Existence of an orthonormal eigenfunction basis

Each fixed boundary-value problem has a complete collection of normal modes, rather than just the first few modes computed above.

Let Ω ⊂ ℝ² be a bounded domain with C∞ boundary. Fix B = D, B = N, or B = Rα with a fixed real constant α. Then AB has compact resolvent. Its spectrum consists of real eigenvalues of finite multiplicity, bounded from below, with no finite accumulation point. There is a real orthonormal basis (uj)j≥1 of L²(Ω), with each uj ∈ C∞(Ω̄), such that

−Δuj=λjBujin Ω∫Ωuiujdx=δij−∞<λ1B≤λ2B≤⋯→+∞-\Delta u_j=\lambda_j^B u_j,\quad \int_\Omega u_i u_j\,dx=\delta_{ij},\quad -\infty<\lambda_1^B\le\lambda_2^B\le\cdots\to+\infty.

Each uj satisfies the chosen boundary condition and the weak identity qB(uj, v) = λjB⟨uj, v⟩ for every v ∈ VB. In particular, for every f ∈ L²(Ω),

f=∑j=1∞⟨f,uj⟩ujin L2(Ω)f=\sum_{j=1}^\infty\langle f,u_j\rangle_{L^2(\Omega)}u_j\quad\text{in }L^2(\Omega).

The same compact-resolvent and basis conclusions hold on a bounded Lipschitz domain, with the boundary conditions interpreted weakly through the forms.

Proof references. [1]; [2], Theorems 3.1 and 3.3. The argument uses the trace inequality, a sufficiently positive shift of the form when needed, and the compact embedding H¹(Ω) ↪ L²(Ω).

Theorem 2. Interior and boundary regularity

Spatial smoothness belongs to the eigenfunctions. Smoothness at the boundary requires stronger hypotheses than smoothness inside the domain.

Let Ω ⊂ ℝ² be a bounded domain with C∞ boundary and let B ∈ {D, N, Rα}, with α ∈ ℝ constant. Every eigenfunction of AB is smooth up to the boundary:

uj∈C∞(Ω¯)for every j≥1u_j\in C^\infty(\overline\Omega)\qquad(j\ge1).

Thus its equation and boundary condition hold classically. The strong operator domains are

D(AD)=H2(Ω)∩H01(Ω)D(Aα)={u∈H2(Ω):∂νu+αu=0on ∂Ω}D(A_D)=H^2(\Omega)\cap H_0^1(\Omega),\quad D(A_\alpha)=\{u\in H^2(\Omega):\partial_\nu u+\alpha u=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega\}.

where the Robin equality is initially an equality of Sobolev traces; Neumann is obtained by setting α = 0. On a bounded Lipschitz domain, eigenfunctions still belong to the form domain and to C∞(Ω). Global H²(Ω) or C∞(Ω̄) regularity is not asserted for general polygonal domains; re-entrant corners can cause singular terms.

Proof references. [3], boundary elliptic estimates followed by bootstrapping; [4], Corollary 4.2, for interior regularity on Lipschitz domains. See also [1], Chapter 5, for passage from weak to classical eigenfunctions.

Theorem 3. Rayleigh and min–max principles

Eigenvalues are extremal energies. This gives a common proof mechanism for comparisons between shapes and boundary parameters.

Let Ω ⊂ ℝ² be a bounded Lipschitz domain and fix B ∈ {D, N, Rα}, α ∈ ℝ. For every integer k ≥ 1,

λkB=minE⊂VB,dimE=kmax0≠u∈EqB(u,u)‖u‖22λ1B=min0≠u∈VBqB(u,u)‖u‖22\lambda_k^B=\min_{E\subset V_B,\dim E=k}\max_{0\ne u\in E}\frac{q_B(u,u)}{\|u\|_2^2},\qquad\lambda_1^B=\min_{0\ne u\in V_B}\frac{q_B(u,u)}{\|u\|_2^2}.

The minimum over k-dimensional subspaces is attained by the span of the first k eigenfunctions. A nonzero function attains the first Rayleigh minimum if and only if it lies in the first eigenspace. These statements also apply when some Robin eigenvalues are negative.

Proof reference. [1], Chapter 10. For a lower-semibounded form, add c‖u‖² to make the form positive, apply the variational principle, and subtract c from the eigenvalues.

Theorem 4. Simplicity, positivity, and signs of the first eigenvalue

Connectedness makes the ground-state eigenspace one-dimensional. It does not force all higher eigenvalues to be simple.

Let Ω ⊂ ℝ² be a bounded connected domain with C∞ boundary. For Dirichlet, Neumann, and Robin conditions with any real constant α, the first eigenvalue is simple, and its real eigenfunction can be chosen strictly positive in Ω. Positivity together with ‖u₁‖₂ = 1 determines this eigenfunction uniquely. Moreover,

λ1D>0,λ1N=0,u1N=A−12λ1(α)<0if α<0;λ1(α)>0if α>0\lambda_1^D>0,\quad\lambda_1^N=0,\quad u_1^N=A^{-1/2};\qquad\lambda_1(\alpha)<0\ (\alpha<0),\quad\lambda_1(\alpha)>0\ (\alpha>0).

For Neumann conditions the zero eigenspace consists exactly of the constant functions. The simplicity and interior-positivity conclusions also hold for bounded connected Lipschitz domains. Higher eigenspaces may have dimension greater than one.

Proof references. [14], Proposition 2.4 and Theorems 4.1 and 4.5, for simplicity and positivity; see also [5], Lemma 6(ii). The Neumann kernel is described in [1], Chapter 5. The sign statements follow from Theorem 3; for α < 0, the constant trial function gives λ₁(α) ≤ αP/A < 0.

Theorem 5. Monotonicity in the Robin parameter and the Dirichlet limit

Increasing the boundary penalty moves the eigenvalues upward. An arbitrarily large positive penalty imposes the Dirichlet constraint in the limit.

Let Ω ⊂ ℝ² be a bounded domain with C∞ boundary. For every j ≥ 1 and all real α ≤ β,

λj(α)≤λj(β)≤λjDwhenever α≤βλj(0)=λjN,limα→+∞λj(α)=λjD\alpha\le\beta\Longrightarrow\lambda_j(\alpha)\le\lambda_j(\beta)\le\lambda_j^D,\qquad\lambda_j(0)=\lambda_j^N,\quad\lim_{\alpha\to+\infty}\lambda_j(\alpha)=\lambda_j^D.

Consequently, for α ≥ 0 one has λjN ≤ λj(α) ≤ λjD. The index j is fixed in the Dirichlet limit, and the parameter convention is ∂νu + αu = 0.

Proof references. The inequalities follow from Theorem 3 and the nested form domains; see [1], Chapter 11. For the limit see [2], §7. The compactness argument uses the bound α∫∂Ω|γu|² dσ ≤ C on low-energy eigenfunctions to force zero trace as α → +∞.

Theorem 6. Analytic dependence and the boundary-mass derivative

A simple eigenvalue varies analytically with the Robin parameter. Ordered higher eigenvalues can change branches at a crossing.

Let Ω ⊂ ℝ² be a bounded domain with C∞ boundary. Suppose λ₀ is a simple eigenvalue of Aα₀, where α₀ ∈ ℝ, with a real normalized eigenfunction u₀. There is an open interval I containing α₀ and real-analytic maps λ : I → ℝ and u : I → H¹(Ω) such that λ(α₀) = λ₀, uα₀ = u₀, Aαuα = λ(α)uα, ‖uα‖₂ = 1, and

dλdα(α)=∫∂Ω|γuα|2dσ>0\lambda\prime(\alpha)=\int_{\partial\Omega}|\gamma u_\alpha|^2\,d\sigma>0,\qquad\|u_\alpha\|_2=1.

For the first eigenvalue, simplicity holds for every real α; hence α ↦ λ₁(α) is real analytic and strictly increasing on ℝ. Analytic eigenvalue branches can be chosen locally through multiple eigenvalues, but their nondecreasing reordering need not be differentiable at crossings.

Proof references. [2], Theorems 4.1 and 4.8; [6], Corollary 7.2, for analytic eigenvectors in the form domain. The strict derivative follows because zero boundary trace together with the Robin condition would give zero Cauchy data and hence the zero eigenfunction.

Theorem 7. Scaling of domains and boundary parameters

A change of size rescales the spectrum, but a Robin parameter has its own inverse-length scale.

Let Ω ⊂ ℝ² be a bounded Lipschitz domain and let s > 0. For every j ≥ 1 and every real constant α,

λjB(sΩ)=s−2λjB(Ω)for B = D or Nλj(sΩ,α)=s−2λj(Ω,sα)\lambda_j^B(s\Omega)=s^{-2}\lambda_j^B(\Omega)\ (B=D,N),\qquad\lambda_j(s\Omega,\alpha)=s^{-2}\lambda_j(\Omega,s\alpha).

Here sΩ = {sx : x ∈ Ω}. The change of variables Usf(x) = s⁻¹f(x/s) is unitary from L²(Ω) to L²(sΩ). Thus keeping the numerical value of α fixed while changing the size is not a pure s⁻² spectral rescaling.

Proof reference. [5], Lemma 6(i), or apply this change of variables directly to the forms and Theorem 3.

Theorem 8. Domain monotonicity for Dirichlet eigenvalues

Enlarging a Dirichlet domain lowers each ordered eigenvalue. This comparison is specific to the Dirichlet form-domain inclusion.

Let Ω₁ ⊂ Ω₂ ⊂ ℝ² be bounded domains. Then

Ω1⊂Ω2⇒λjD(Ω2)≤λjD(Ω1)(j≥1)\Omega_1\subset\Omega_2\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\lambda_j^D(\Omega_2)\le\lambda_j^D(\Omega_1)\quad(j\ge1).

For this statement, Dirichlet eigenvalues are defined by the form on H¹₀, so boundary smoothness is unnecessary. No corresponding general domain-inclusion inequality for the Neumann or fixed-parameter Robin spectrum is claimed here.

Proof reference. [1], Theorem 11.2. Extension by zero embeds H¹₀(Ω₁) into H¹₀(Ω₂) without changing the Rayleigh quotient; then apply min–max.

Theorem 9. Weyl’s law in two dimensions

The leading growth of the high-energy spectrum determines the area. The leading term is the same for the three boundary conditions.

Let Ω ⊂ ℝ² be a bounded domain with C∞ boundary and area A. Fix B = D, B = N, or B = Rα with α ∈ ℝ fixed independently of the energy. If NB(Λ) = #{j ≥ 1 : λjB ≤ Λ}, then

NB(Λ)=A4πΛ+o(Λ)(Λ→+∞)limj→∞λjBj=4πAN_B(\Lambda)=\frac{A}{4\pi}\Lambda+o(\Lambda),\qquad\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{\lambda_j^B}{j}=\frac{4\pi}{A}.

Finite low-energy or negative Robin eigenvalues do not change this leading asymptotic. The statement alone gives no error estimate for the first 12 numerical modes.

Proof references. [1], Chapter 12, for Dirichlet and Neumann. For arbitrary real Robin parameters, combine their asymptotics with the trace/min–max comparison in [13], Proposition 4.1, and the upper bound in Theorem 5.

Theorem 10. The Berezin–Li–Yau eigenvalue-sum inequality

Area alone gives a universal lower bound for sums of Dirichlet eigenvalues. Its sharpness is asymptotic rather than an equality attained by one bounded shape at a finite index.

Let Ω ⊂ ℝ² be a nonempty bounded open set of area A, and let its Dirichlet eigenvalues be ordered with multiplicity. For every integer k ≥ 1,

∑j=1kλjD≥2πAk2(k≥1)\sum_{j=1}^k\lambda_j^D\ge\frac{2\pi}{A}k^2\qquad(k\ge1).

For a bounded domain the inequality is strict for every finite k. For each fixed bounded smooth domain,

limk→∞∑j=1kλjD2πAk2=1\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{j=1}^k\lambda_j^D}{(2\pi/A)k^2}=1.

The inequality and strictness do not assert the same bound for Neumann or Robin eigenvalues.

Proof references. [7], the Dirichlet eigenvalue-sum estimate; [8], the positive moment-of-inertia remainder, which implies finite-index strictness. The limiting ratio follows from Theorem 9 by summation.

Theorem 11. Faber–Krahn inequalities and equality cases

For the lowest eigenvalue, the disk gives an attained equality case in the Dirichlet and positive-Robin area comparisons.

Let Ω ⊂ ℝ² be a bounded domain with C∞ boundary, and let BR be the disk centred at the origin with πR² = |Ω|. Then

λ1D(Ω)≥λ1D(BR)=j0,12R2λ1(Ω,α)≥λ1(BR,α)for each fixed α>0\lambda_1^D(\Omega)\ge\lambda_1^D(B_R)=j_{0,1}^2/R^2,\qquad\lambda_1(\Omega,\alpha)\ge\lambda_1(B_R,\alpha)\quad(\alpha>0).

In each of these inequalities, equality holds if and only if Ω is a translate of BR. Here j0,1 is the first positive zero of the Bessel function J₀. The Robin comparison uses the same positive value of α on both domains. At α = 0 all connected domains have first eigenvalue zero, so disk rigidity disappears. The Robin assertion above is restricted to α > 0.

Proof references. [9], for the Robin inequality; [10], for equality and the Dirichlet case. The Dirichlet disk eigenvalue also follows by separation in polar coordinates; see [1], Chapter 2.

Theorem 12. Courant’s nodal-domain bound

The position of an eigenvalue in the ordered spectrum bounds the number of sign regions of each corresponding eigenfunction.

Let Ω ⊂ ℝ² be a bounded domain with C∞ boundary and fix Dirichlet, Neumann, or Robin conditions with α ∈ ℝ. If u is a nonzero real eigenfunction associated with λkB, let n(u) be the number of connected components of the open set {x ∈ Ω : u(x) ≠ 0}. Then

n(u)≤kif −Δu=λkBun(u)\le k\quad\text{if }-\Delta u=\lambda_k^B u\text{ and }u\ne0.

Eigenvalues are counted with multiplicity. The bound applies to negative Robin eigenvalues as well. It is an upper bound, not a claim that every k-th mode has exactly k nodal domains; pixel-level colour regions are not a rigorous nodal-domain count.

Proof references. [11], the general variational nodal-domain theorem; [13], §1, for the Robin formulation including real, sign-changing boundary parameters. The proof combines nodal truncation with min–max and unique continuation.

Theorem 13. Noncongruent isospectral planar domains exist

A complete list of Dirichlet frequencies need not determine the geometry of a planar drum.

There exist bounded, simply connected polygonal domains Ω₁, Ω₂ ⊂ ℝ² that are not related by any Euclidean isometry and nevertheless satisfy

λjD(Ω1)=λjD(Ω2)for every j≥1\lambda_j^D(\Omega_1)=\lambda_j^D(\Omega_2)\qquad\text{for every }j\ge1.

The equality includes all multiplicities and the entire infinite spectrum. This existence statement uses polygonal, hence Lipschitz, domains; it does not assert an example with C∞ boundary or an example for arbitrary Robin parameters. A finite set of approximately matching eigenvalues does not establish this property.

Proof reference. [12], the construction and proof by Gordon, Webb, and Wolpert. No exact isospectral pair is currently implemented in the drawing tool.

Open problems and conjectures about Laplacian eigenvalues

These questions concern the continuum operators defined above. Eigenvalues are ordered with multiplicity, starting at j = 1; λjD denotes the Dirichlet spectrum. Each entry gives an interpretation, a precise question, a dated status, and references to the known results and recent claims.

A preliminary question: explicit formulas for general triangles

Explicit Dirichlet spectra are classical for equilateral, right isosceles, and 30°–60°–90° triangles. No comparable general explicit formula is known for arbitrary triangles. However, “closed formula” does not specify a class of permitted functions or operations, so it is a research goal rather than a single precise conjecture. Problem 2 below is a sharply formulated triangle question. See [O1], §1.

Open problem 1. Hearing smooth or convex drums

The complete list of vibration frequencies need not determine a polygonal drum. Whether smoothness or convexity restores uniqueness is unknown.

Smooth-domain question. Let Ω₁, Ω₂ ⊂ ℝ² be bounded connected domains with C∞ boundaries. Is the following implication true?

λjD(Ω1)=λjD(Ω2)for every j≥1⟹Ω2=QΩ1+afor some Q∈O(2),a∈ℝ2\lambda_j^D(\Omega_1)=\lambda_j^D(\Omega_2)\ (j\ge1)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\Omega_2=Q\Omega_1+a\text{ for some }Q\in O(2),\ a\in\mathbb R^2?

Here O(2) is the group of orthogonal matrices; rotations, reflections, and translations are allowed. Convex-domain question. Is the same implication true if both domains are bounded and convex, without requiring smooth boundaries? The smooth strictly convex case is already an unresolved special case.

Status: open in both forms. The known polygonal Dirichlet-isospectral counterexamples do not resolve these restrictions. Spectral uniqueness is known for certain special families, including sufficiently small-eccentricity ellipses. Results for Steklov spectra concern a different boundary-value problem.

References and status. [O2], Open Problem 6.2.26; [O3], §6, Outlook. No subsequent general resolution was located in the literature check.

Conjecture 2. Hearing a triangle from its first three tones

A triangle has only three geometric parameters up to rigid motions. Can just three eigenvalues determine all of them?

Let T₁, T₂ ⊂ ℝ² be nondegenerate Euclidean triangular domains. Does equality of their first three Dirichlet eigenvalues imply congruence?

λjD(T1)=λjD(T2)(j=1,2,3)⟹T2=QT1+afor some Q∈O(2),a∈ℝ2\lambda_j^D(T_1)=\lambda_j^D(T_2)\ (j=1,2,3)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad T_2=QT_1+a\text{ for some }Q\in O(2),\ a\in\mathbb R^2?

No equal-area assumption is imposed: scale is part of the geometry to be recovered. Repeated eigenvalues are included in the ordering.

Status: open. The full Dirichlet spectrum determines a triangle within the class of triangles. Gómez-Serrano and Orriols proved the existence of noncongruent triangles with identical λ₁, λ₂, λ₄; their result does not concern the triple λ₁, λ₂, λ₃. Recent triangle shape inequalities do not settle this inverse problem.

References and status. [O4], §1; [O5], Introduction and Theorem 1.1. No proof or counterexample for the first-three conjecture was located in the literature check.

Conjecture 3. The disk and the third Dirichlet eigenvalue

Faber–Krahn identifies the optimal shape for the first eigenvalue. Global optimization is already unresolved for the third.

Let Ω ⊂ ℝ² be a nonempty bounded open set of area A, and let B be a disk of area A. Is

λ3D(Ω)≥λ3D(B)=πj1,12A\lambda_3^D(\Omega)\ge\lambda_3^D(B)=\frac{\pi j_{1,1}^2}{A}?

always true? Here j1,1 is the first positive zero of the Bessel function J₁. Multiplicity matters: the disk has λ₂D(B) = λ₃D(B). No uniqueness claim for an optimizer is included in this question.

Status: open as a global inequality. A March 2026 research article still identifies this optimization as conjectural. Numerical optimization and perturbative statements near a disk do not establish the inequality for every domain.

Reference and status. [O6], §1.1, which points to Henrot’s Open Problem 8. No subsequent global proof or counterexample was located.

Conjecture 4. The polygonal Pólya–Szegő inequality

If the number of sides is prescribed, the natural candidate for the lowest fundamental frequency is the regular polygon.

Fix an integer n ≥ 5 and an area A > 0. Let Rn be a regular n-gon of area A. For every simple planar n-gon P of area A, is

λ1D(P)≥λ1D(Rn)\lambda_1^D(P)\ge\lambda_1^D(R_n)?

true, with equality only when P is congruent to Rn? Convexity is not imposed on P. The analogous assertions for n = 3, 4 are established.

Status: open in this full form; global pentagon and hexagon cases remain open. Bogosel and Bucur established local minimality for regular pentagons and hexagons using interval arithmetic to control roundoff. Their earlier computations for more side counts should not be mistaken for fully validated global proofs.

Recent preprint claim. Cheng, Gui, Hu, and Li (September 2026), Theorem 1.1, assert the global inequality and uniqueness for convex polygons with at most N sides whenever N ≥ N₀ for some sufficiently large N₀. This restricted large-N statement does not resolve the global n = 5, 6 questions or the full nonconvex conjecture.

References and status. [O7], reduction to finitely many certified computations; [O8], validated local minimality; [O9], Theorem 1.1 (preprint). No full resolution was located.

Conjecture 5. Bareket’s question for simply connected planar domains

An attractive Robin boundary can concentrate a ground state near the edge. The optimal shape depends on the geometric constraint and the allowed topology.

Let Ω ⊂ ℝ² be a bounded simply connected domain with C∞ boundary, let α < 0 be fixed, and let B be a disk with |B| = |Ω|. With the convention ∂νu + αu = 0, is

λ1(Ω,α)≤λ1(B,α)(α<0)\lambda_1(\Omega,\alpha)\le\lambda_1(B,\alpha)\qquad(\alpha<0)?

always true? Both domains use the same value of α. The area is fixed; the perimeter is not.

Status: open for all negative parameters in this class. Removing simple connectedness makes the fixed-area assertion false: annuli can exceed the disk for sufficiently negative α. The fixed-perimeter comparison in the plane is an established theorem and is a different constraint.

Recent partial progress. Gavitone, Krejčiřík, and Paoli’s June 2026 preprint, Theorem 1, treats bounded convex domains for α ∈ [α₀, 0], where α₀ < 0 depends only on area and dimension. It does not cover every negative α or every simply connected domain.

References and status. [O10], §5.3, Conjecture 2; [O11], §1.2; [O12], Theorem 1 (preprint). No full resolution was located.

Conjecture 6. Ivrii’s periodic-billiard conjecture

High-frequency waves are related to rays reflecting inside the domain. An unresolved question about periodic rays controls a finer version of Weyl’s law.

Consider a bounded planar domain Ω with C∞ boundary, strictly convex with positive boundary curvature. Let Σ = ∂Ω × (−π/2, π/2) be the billiard phase space: s is boundary arclength and θ the outgoing angle measured from the inward normal. Let T : Σ → Σ take a ray to its next reflected boundary state, and give Σ the invariant measure dμ = cos θ ds dθ. Is the set of periodic states of measure zero?

μ(⋃q=1∞{z∈Σ:Tqz=z})=0\mu\!\left(\bigcup_{q=1}^{\infty}\{z\in\Sigma:T^qz=z\}\right)=0?

This is an already-open special case of the conjecture for general smooth bounded Euclidean domains, with grazing trajectories excluded.

Related spectral question. For any bounded smooth planar domain, write A = |Ω|, P = |∂Ω|, and ND(Λ) = #{j : λjD(Ω) < Λ}. Is the unsmoothed expansion

ND(Λ)=A4πΛ−P4πΛ+o(Λ)N_D(\Lambda)=\frac{A}{4\pi}\Lambda-\frac{P}{4\pi}\sqrt\Lambda+o(\sqrt\Lambda)\qquad(\Lambda\to+\infty).

valid as Λ → +∞ without any extra dynamical hypothesis?

Status: open in general. Ivrii proved the two-term expansion under the periodic-set measure-zero assumption; that assumption is sufficient, and no equivalence is asserted here. Frank and Larson’s unconditional two-term results for positive-order Riesz means concern an averaged spectrum, rather than this counting function.

References and status. [O13], §1, formula (4) and its discussion; [O14], p.19, for the conventional billiard conjecture and period-specific progress. No general resolution was located.

Conjecture 7. Pólya’s pointwise Dirichlet inequality

Weyl’s law gives the leading behavior at high energy. Pólya asks whether its coefficient already bounds every individual Dirichlet eigenvalue.

Let Ω ⊂ ℝ² be a nonempty bounded open set of area A, with Dirichlet eigenvalues defined on H¹₀(Ω). Is

AλjD(Ω)≥4πjfor every j≥1A\lambda_j^D(\Omega)\ge4\pi j\qquad(j\ge1)?

always true? No simple-connectedness or boundary-smoothness assumption is imposed. This pointwise assertion is stronger than the eigenvalue-sum estimate in Theorem 10: it would imply ∑j=1kλjD ≥ (2π/A)k(k + 1).

Status: open in full generality; a simply connected case has a recent claimed proof. Established special cases include tiling domains, disks and sectors, and Dirichlet annuli. The general-domain statement above is not resolved by those results.

Recent preprint claim, 9 October 2026. Tang and Wang, Theorem 1.2, assert the strict inequality |Ω|λjD(Ω) > 4πj for every bounded nonempty simply connected planar domain. Their Remark 1.4 gives some additional domains with small holes, rather than arbitrary multiply connected domains. The cited version is a preprint; no independent specialist assessment or refereed publication was located in this check. Its Neumann companion is described there as in preparation. The claim is therefore recorded separately from the established theorems above.

References and status. [O15], introduction and annulus theorem; [O16], Conjecture 1.1, Theorem 1.2, and Remarks 1.3–1.4 (preprint). The unresolved statement here permits arbitrary topology.

Numerical method and conventions

The Dirichlet operator uses the five-point finite-difference stencil on strictly interior grid points. The Robin approximation uses a symmetric grid energy with polygon-arclength quadrature and boundary-trace elimination. This is an educational approximation rather than a fitted-boundary finite-element method. Corners, narrow necks, and strongly negative α can produce substantial boundary error, even when the discrete eigenpair residual is small.

The displayed residual measures ‖Au − λu‖₂ / (max(|λ|, 1) ‖u‖₂) for the discrete matrix. It indicates solver convergence, not accuracy of the continuous boundary approximation. Increase the grid resolution under Advanced settings to compare results. Negative Robin eigenvalues are retained; their time dependence in the wave equation is non-oscillatory, so their animation and individual sound controls are disabled.

Calculations stay in your browser. The page also works offline.

Numerical eigensolver

The first 12 eigenpairs are computed by block inverse iteration with two-pass modified Gram–Schmidt orthogonalization, Rayleigh–Ritz extraction, and a Jacobi eigensolver for the projected matrix. Each inverse application uses conjugate gradients with an incomplete-Cholesky preconditioner.

A Gershgorin lower bound supplies a shift for the Robin problem so that inverse solves remain positive definite. The shift is removed from the reported eigenvalues, including the zero Neumann eigenvalue and negative attractive-Robin eigenvalues.

The iteration stops when the maximum scaled residual is below 2 × 10⁻⁵, or after 80 steps (140 for negative Robin parameters). An unconverged result is explicitly labelled. See the visible numerical-method section for the distinction between this residual and boundary approximation error.
