Solutions of the nonlinear Schrödinger equation

The nonlinear Schrödinger equation (NLS) describes the evolution of a complex wave amplitude. In one spatial dimension, cubic NLS balances dispersion with a nonlinear phase rotation. The focusing sign supports bright solitons; the defocusing sign spreads localized waves.

Theorems about cubic NLS

On ℝ and on the circle

Each entry gives an interpretation, a precise statement and a proof or proof references. The sign convention is always i ut + uxx + 2σ|u|²u = 0. Results about the continuum equation do not certify a finite numerical computation.

Set-up and notation

Let X = ℝ or 𝕋L = ℝ/Lℤ. The unknown u is complex valued; σ = +1 means focusing and σ = −1 means defocusing. Write Hs(X) for the L² Sobolev space, with Fourier weight (1 + |ξ|²)s/2. Frequencies are ξ ∈ ℝ on the line and ξ = 2πn/L on the circle. The weighted space H1,1(ℝ) consists of f ∈ H¹ with xf ∈ L².

u(t) = S(t)u₀ + 2iσ∫₀t S(t − r)(|u(r)|²u(r)) dr, S(t) = eit∂x².

For classical solutions the cubic term is ordinary multiplication. Below L², a continuous extension of the smooth flow and an arbitrary distributional solution are different notions. On the line the scaling uλ(t,x) = λu(λ²t,λx) has critical Sobolev index sc = −½. Thus the one-dimensional cubic equation is mass subcritical for both signs.

Theorem 1. The Schrödinger group is unitary ℝ and 𝕋L

Each Fourier coefficient rotates; its modulus stays fixed.

For every s ∈ ℝ, S(t) is a strongly continuous unitary group on Hs(X). With f̂(ξ) = ∫ e−ixξf(x) dx on ℝ,

(S(t)f)̂(ξ) = e−itξ²f̂(ξ), ‖S(t)f‖Hs = ‖f‖Hs.

The function v(t) = S(t)f is the unique solution of i vt + vxx = 0 in C(ℝ;Hs). Its dispersion relation is ω(ξ) = ξ² and its group velocity is 2ξ. In particular, positive and negative carrier frequencies travel in opposite directions.

Proof. Fourier transformation reduces the equation to ∂tv̂ = −iξ²v̂. Plancherel gives unitarity, dominated convergence gives continuity, and the scalar ODE gives uniqueness. Fourier series gives the circle statement.

Theorem 2. Decay on the line, revivals on the circle Geometry matters

Spreading to infinity and recurrent periodic motion are different phenomena.

On ℝ, for t ≠ 0 and f ∈ L¹ ∩ L², the oscillatory kernel formula and its dispersive estimate are

S(t)f(x) = (4πit)−½∫ℝ ei(x−y)²/(4t)f(y) dy,
‖S(t)f‖L∞ ≤ (4π|t|)−½‖f‖L¹.

The square root is chosen with the usual Fresnel phase. On 𝕋L, every initial datum revives at

S(TL)f = f, TL = L²/(2π).

There is consequently no global decaying L¹ → L∞ estimate of the same kind on the circle, even after discarding the zero mode.

Proof. Evaluate the inverse Fourier transform of e−itξ² and take its absolute value. On the circle, exp(−iTL(2πn/L)²) = exp(−2πin²) = 1. A nonzero smooth function at successive revivals rules out decay.

Theorem 3. Global well-posedness and the sharp line threshold Both signs

Smooth solutions never blow up here; the line also admits a canonical flow below L².

On ℝ and 𝕋L, cubic NLS is globally well-posed in L², with persistence in Hs for s ≥ 0. Uniqueness in this assertion uses the standard solution class produced by the construction.

On ℝ, for each s > −½, the classical Schwartz flow extends uniquely to a jointly continuous map

Φ : ℝ × Hs(ℝ) → Hs(ℝ).

Equivalently, smooth approximations converging in Hs produce the same limit in C([−T,T];Hs) for every finite T. This is canonical extension uniqueness. At s = −½, norm inflation obstructs a continuous flow of this kind. The corresponding ordinary periodic equation does not inherit the negative Sobolev theory.

Proof references. Tsutsumi and Bourgain for the classical L² theories [1]; Harrop-Griffiths–Killip–Vişan, Theorem 1.1, for the sharp line result and the endpoint obstruction [2].

Theorem 4. Unconditional uniqueness in Hs, s ≥ ⅙ ℝ and 𝕋L · both signs

At this regularity no extra space–time norm is needed to identify the solution.

Let s ≥ ⅙ and T > 0. If u,v ∈ C([−T,T];Hs(X)) solve cubic NLS distributionally with the same initial data, then

u(t) = v(t) for all t ∈ [−T,T].

The embedding H⅙(X) ⊂ L³(X) makes |u|²u locally integrable in space and time. “Unconditional” specifies uniqueness in the full stated class. It does not assert that an arbitrary L² curve has a well-defined cubic term, nor that all negative-regularity notions of solution coincide.

Proof references. Guo–Kwon–Oh on the circle and Kwon–Oh–Yoon on the line [3]. Their infinite normal-form reductions recover estimates directly in the stated class. The threshold here is a verified theorem, not a claim of optimality for every possible solution concept.

Theorem 5. Conservation laws control H¹ globally ℝ and 𝕋L · both signs

Focusing NLS in one-dimensional cubic form has solitons, but no finite-time H¹ blow-up.

For H¹ solutions, the following quantities are independent of time:

M = ∫X|u|² dx, P = Im ∫Xūux dx,
E = ∫X(|ux|² − σ|u|⁴) dx.

In particular, supt∈ℝ ‖u(t)‖H¹ < ∞, with a bound depending on M, E and, on the circle, L. For σ = −1 the energy is positive. For σ = +1 the one-dimensional Gagliardo–Nirenberg estimate gives

‖u‖₄⁴ ≤ C‖u‖₂³‖ux‖₂ on ℝ,

with an additional CL‖u‖₂⁴ term on the circle. The resulting quadratic bound controls ‖ux‖₂.

Proof. Differentiate the three integrals for smooth solutions, substitute ut = i(uxx + 2σ|u|²u), and integrate by parts. Density extends the identities to H¹. The inequality above and the continuation criterion yield the global bound.

Theorem 6. Bright solitons and plane waves Sign and background matter

A fixed envelope does not imply that the complex field is stationary.

For focusing NLS on ℝ, A > 0 and k,x₀,θ ∈ ℝ give the exact bright soliton

u(t,x) = A sech(A(x − x₀ − 2kt))
× exp(i[k(x − x₀) + (A² − k²)t + θ]).

Its velocity is 2k and its mass is 2A. For either sign, the exact plane wave is

u(t,x) = A exp(i[kx − (k² − 2σA²)t + θ]).

It is L-periodic when kL ∈ 2πℤ. On ℝ a nonzero plane wave has infinite mass. Defocusing NLS has dark solitons on nonzero backgrounds, which belong to a different phase space from decaying L² data. The simulator’s bright pulse on the circle is a periodized line profile; only its line formula is exact.

Proof. Substitute the formulas, using (sech z)″ = sech z − 2 sech³z. The plane-wave frequency follows immediately. Translation, phase rotation and the Galilean transformation account for x₀, θ and k.

Theorem 7. Focusing plane waves are modulationally unstable Linearized statement · 𝕋L

A small long-wave modulation can grow even though mass stays constant.

Linearize about u = A e2iσA²t, A > 0, by writing u = e2iσA²t(A + a + ib). At first order,

at = −bxx, bt = axx + 4σA²a.

A mode of nonzero frequency κ = 2πn/L has exponential growth parameter λ satisfying

λ² = κ²(4σA² − κ²).

For σ = +1, precisely the nonzero modes |κ| < 2A have a growing linearized solution. For σ = −1, all nonzero mode eigenvalues are purely imaginary. This linearized distinction does not by itself classify the later nonlinear evolution.

Proof. Insert a,b proportional to exp(λt + iκx) in the two real equations and compute the determinant. The perturbed-plane-wave preset uses A = 1 and κ = 8π/40, which lies in the unstable focusing band.

Theorem 8. The Zakharov–Shabat zero-curvature representation ℝ and 𝕋L · both signs

NLS is the compatibility condition of two linear systems with a spectral parameter.

For smooth u define J = diag(1,−1) and the following 2 × 2 matrices, with λ a spectral parameter:

Q =
0u
−σū0
, U = −iλJ + Q,
V = −2iλ²J + 2λQ + iJQx − iJQ².

The systems ψx = Uψ and ψt = Vψ are compatible precisely when

Ut − Vx + [U,V] = 0,

whose off-diagonal entry is cubic NLS. For periodic smooth solutions the monodromy spectrum is invariant; for suitable decaying data the associated scattering data evolve explicitly. A Lax pair supplies structure, while invertible spectral coordinates require additional analysis.

Proof and reference. Use JQ = −QJ and Q² = −σ|u|²Id to expand the compatibility condition. The spectral formulation originates with Zakharov–Shabat [4].

Theorem 9. Localized small data have modified scattering ℝ · both signs

Radiation decays like a linear wave, but accumulates a logarithmic phase.

For sufficiently small Schwartz data in the weighted norm ‖u₀‖H¹ + ‖xu₀‖₂, there is an asymptotic profile W such that, as t → +∞,

u(t,x) = (2t)−½ eix²/(4t) W(x/(2t))
× exp(iσ|W(x/(2t))|² log t) + oL∞(t−½).

The normalization of W absorbs the constant Fresnel phase. The correction exp(iσ|W|² log t) is the long-range cubic effect. Ordinary linear scattering is therefore not the appropriate generic description in this one-dimensional cubic setting. This theorem assumes localization and smallness; it does not cover every L² datum.

Proof reference. Ifrim–Tataru [5], small-data asymptotic expansion, translated to the coefficient 2σ and ray variable x/(2t). Along each ray the leading amplitude satisfies ∂tWray ≈ iσt−1|Wray|²Wray, which explains the phase.

Theorem 10. Soliton asymptotics for generic localized focusing data ℝ · focusing

Discrete spectral data produce coherent waves; the continuous part produces radiation.

For u₀ ∈ H1,1(ℝ) whose Zakharov–Shabat data have finitely many simple discrete eigenvalues and no spectral singularities, the focusing solution admits a uniform long-time expansion in each fixed cone x₁ + v₁t ≤ x ≤ x₂ + v₂t:

u = usol + urad + O(t−¾), urad = O(t−½).

The leading reflectionless solution usol is determined by the discrete spectrum in the cone, with parameters modified by interactions. The radiation carries a nonlinear phase. This is a cone-wise expansion with explicit spectral hypotheses, not a theorem about all H¹ data. Eigenvalues with equal velocities can form bound multisolitons, so a universal picture of separated single pulses is too restrictive.

Proof reference. Borghese–Jenkins–McLaughlin [6], long-time expansion by nonlinear steepest descent. Their coefficient convention is converted by rescaling time and amplitude.

Theorem 11. Stability of the multisoliton manifold ℝ · focusing

A nearby solution stays close to a suitably adjusted coherent solution.

Fix N ≥ 1, s > −½ and a compact set K in the open upper spectral half-plane. The pure N-soliton states form a smooth 4N-dimensional manifold ℳN. There exist ε₀,C > 0, uniform for spectral parameters in K, such that if v₀ ∈ ℳN has its spectral parameters in K and

‖w₀ − v₀‖Hs = ε ≤ ε₀,

then there is another pure N-soliton solution ṽ with

supt∈ℝ ‖w(t) − ṽ(t)‖Hs ≤ Cε.

No separation of the eigenvalues is required. The comparison soliton may have adjusted spectral data. Stability does not assert convergence as t → ∞.

Proof reference. Koch–Tataru [7], Theorems 1.5 and 8.1, using soliton addition and removal maps.

Theorem 12. Global Birkhoff coordinates and recurrence 𝕋L · defocusing

Periodic defocusing NLS is a nonlinear collection of rotations.

For s ≥ 0, defocusing NLS has compatible global canonical Birkhoff coordinates Ω : Hs(𝕋L;ℂ) → hs(ℤ;ℂ), where hs has squared norm ∑n(1 + n²)s|zn|². In these coordinates, with a choice of orientation,

In = |zn|²/2, zn(t) = e−iωn(I)tzn(0).

The actions are conserved. Every orbit is relatively compact in Hs, and t ↦ u(t) is Bohr almost periodic: for each ε > 0, relatively dense time shifts τ satisfy

supt∈ℝ ‖u(t + τ) − u(t)‖Hs < ε.

These are nonlinear recurrence statements, without a common exact period. The global defocusing coordinate theorem is not a global focusing theorem.

Proof reference. Grébert–Kappeler and Molnar [8]. Fixed actions give uniformly small sequence tails and rotations on finite-dimensional tori; continuity of Ω−1 gives compactness and almost periodicity.

Open questions about NLS and its relatives

Literature checked 7 November 2026

Integrability settles many one-dimensional questions under suitable hypotheses. It does not turn every rough-data or large-time problem into an explicit formula. The first two questions concern one-dimensional cubic NLS; the third changes dimension and is not simulated above.

These entries distinguish a research problem from a theorem. “No resolution located” describes this literature check, not a guarantee that no new result exists. Recent preprint claims are labelled separately below.

One-dimensional focusing cubic NLS · ℝ · research program

1. Soliton resolution without weighted genericity hypotheses

A sharp global flow is available far beyond the data classes of classical asymptotic formulas.

Give a complete asymptotic description for general finite-energy focusing data u₀ ∈ H¹(ℝ), without assuming xu₀ ∈ L² or the simple, nonsingular scattering data used in Theorem 10. Identify persistent coherent components, their interactions and the modified dispersive remainder, in a topology that also records the mass carried by radiation.

One must allow bound multisolitons with equal velocities and handle exceptional or accumulating spectral data. Accordingly, a statement that every solution is a finite sum of separated sech pulses plus a freely scattering remainder would be an unsuitable universal conjecture.

Known and remaining. Generic H1,1 cone asymptotics and multisoliton stability are established. Global H¹ existence is established for both signs. These results do not supply the stated unrestricted asymptotic classification. No complete resolution in this class was located.

Sources. Borghese–Jenkins–McLaughlin [6]; Koch–Tataru [7]; the discussion of the restricted inverse-scattering setting in Ifrim–Tataru [R1].

One-dimensional renormalized cubic NLS · circle · open Sobolev problem

2. A flow on the whole negative Sobolev space

Removing a divergent mean phase helps, but Fourier–Lebesgue well-posedness does not cover every negative Sobolev datum.

For each fixed −½ < s < 0, does the renormalized equation on 𝕋L, initially defined for smooth functions by

i ut + uxx + 2σ(|u|² − 2m(u))u = 0,
m(u) = L−1∫𝕋L|u|² dx,

admit a unique continuous extension of the smooth flow to all of Hs(𝕋L)? For a general negative Sobolev distribution, m(u) and |u|²u are not separately defined; the renormalized expression must be interpreted through a limiting or normal-form formulation.

Known and remaining. Oh–Wang obtain global well-posedness in almost critical Fourier–Lebesgue spaces ℱLp, p < ∞, through normal forms. Those spaces do not contain the whole Hs space at a fixed negative s. The sharp Hs(ℝ) theorem is a line result. Neither statement answers this periodic Sobolev question; no full resolution was located.

Sources. Oh–Wang’s introduction and subsequent normal-form paper [R2]; comparison with the line theorem [2]. This asks about the renormalized flow, not the ordinary periodic equation, which already has an obstruction below L².

Beyond the simulation · defocusing cubic NLS · two-dimensional torus

3. Can one orbit have an unbounded higher Sobolev norm?

Arbitrarily large finite growth across different solutions need not produce a single infinitely growing orbit.

For the standard equation i ut + Δu − 2|u|²u = 0 on 𝕋², construct a global solution with u(0) ∈ Hs(𝕋²), for some s > 1, such that

limsupt→+∞ ‖u(t)‖Hs = ∞.

Mass and energy still control H¹. The question asks whether one solution can transfer enough energy to successively higher frequencies to make a stronger norm unbounded.

Known and remaining. Colliander–Keel–Staffilani–Takaoka–Tao construct arbitrarily large finite growth. Unbounded trajectories are known for certain modified nonlinearities and other geometries; these do not settle standard cubic NLS on 𝕋². The one-dimensional defocusing circle flow in Theorem 12 instead has bounded Hs orbits. No solution of the stated two-dimensional problem was located in the current check.

Sources. The growth construction, Hani’s distinction between polynomial and modified nonlinearities, and a 2025 paper reiterating Bourgain’s question [R3].

What the numerical experiment computes

Fourier splitting on a finite grid

A symmetric Strang step applies half a nonlinear phase rotation, the exact discrete Fourier multiplier exp(−iξ²Δt), and another half rotation. There is no artificial damping. The method preserves discrete mass up to roundoff; energy and momentum drift help diagnose error. It is second order in time for smooth resolved solutions. No finite grid resolves arbitrary high-frequency transfer.

The default uses Δt = 0.004 and 512 points across a window of length 40. A refined time step and higher resolution are independent checks; the animation speed is unrelated to Δt. The page pauses if significant mass reaches unresolved frequencies.

Circle versus padded line

The circle is computed with periodic endpoints x = ±20. The line mode keeps the same visible window inside a periodic box of length 320, 640 or 1,280, with proportionally more points. There is no boundary condition at infinity in the mathematical line problem; padding only delays finite-box effects.

Localized presets are placed in the larger box. Plane-wave presets are available only on the circle. Waves can leave the visible window while the polar plot still displays only that window. A boundary diagnostic pauses a line run if appreciable mass reaches the outer box. Conservation diagnostics always use the full computational box.
